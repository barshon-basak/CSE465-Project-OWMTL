# M12_v2 — Backbone Re-Decision Justification

**Date:** 2026-08-29 · **Supersedes:** M12 (2026-08-03)
**Decided on:** `icbhi_score_official` · **Split:** `official_icbhi_60_40_patient_disjoint`
**Test set:** 2756 cycles / 49 patients · inference only, no training

> Generated by `m12_v2_backbone_redecision.py`. Every number is computed from the frozen
> checkpoints at run time, so this document cannot drift from the models it describes.

## Why M12 was re-run

M12 selected M2 as the shared backbone. Three problems with that decision:

1. it used the **macro** `icbhi_score`, which the 2026-08-13 audit showed inflates by
   +0.11 on average and masks Se/Sp pathologies;
2. every candidate number came from the **11-patient / 492-cycle fallback split**;
3. a 0.0243 margin was compared against a hand-set 0.0129 tolerance — **no confidence
   interval, no paired test**.

## Result

| candidate | architecture | official ICBHI | 95% CI | Se | Sp | macro-F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 | scratch CNN depth=4 width=32 | 0.472 | [0.4021, 0.5344] | 0.2005 | 0.7435 | 0.2884 |
| M3 | mobilenet_v3_small (feat_dim 576) | **0.5135** | [0.4796, 0.5433] | 0.198 | 0.829 | 0.3495 |

**Paired:** M2 − M3 = **-0.0415**, 95% CI **[-0.0988, 0.0128]**, p = 0.1484
(patient-level bootstrap, the resampling unit that respects non-independence of cycles).

McNemar: M2-only-right 259, M3-only-right 391,
p = 2.533e-07 — **cycle-level and anti-conservative**; it treats
2756 cycles from 49 patients as independent. Lead with the bootstrap.

## Decision

**NO SEPARATION. M3 leads on the point estimate (0.5135 vs 0.472) but the paired interval spans zero, so the candidates are not distinguishable on this test set. Any backbone choice between them must be justified on grounds other than the official score — efficiency, or a stated preference — and said so explicitly.**

## Decision status: ACCEPTED (2026-08-29)

**Selected: M3 (mobilenet_v3_small) — as a stated preference, not a measured win.**

The paired interval spans zero. M3 is preferred because it leads on the point estimate and
on macro-F1. Write it that way. "M3 outperforms M2" is not supported by this evidence.

## Why the ORIGINAL backbone question was not answered here

M12 selected the 5-block/width-48 CNN in `Asif's/M2/M2_best_model.pth` — the checkpoint
M13, M15, M17, M29 and the concept engine all inherit. That choice is still unverified on a
corrected split, and it **cannot** be verified by re-running the existing checkpoints:

> The original M2/M3 trained under the fallback rule (`patient_id <= 111 -> test`), so they
> trained on all 115 patients with id > 111. **45 of the 49 patients in the official test
> set — 92% — were in their training data.** Scoring those checkpoints on the corrected
> test set would measure memorisation.

Settling it requires **retraining** M2 and M3 on the corrected split. Deferred: two
independent comparisons at ~49 test patients have now produced deltas of 0.040 and 0.042,
both with intervals spanning zero, so a third is unlikely to separate anything — and four
RTK requirements currently have no implementation at all.

That 92% figure is worth keeping. It is the concrete measure of how far the silent split
fallback contaminated the model zoo, and it is sharper than "11 test patients".

## What this does and does not settle

**Does:** on the corrected split, with a proper interval, these two candidates are not
separable on the official metric. A 0.04 point-estimate gap at 49 test patients is inside
the noise. M3 is preferable on the point estimate and on macro-F1, and is the reasonable
default — but it must be stated as a preference, not as a measured win.

**Does not:** this compares the 2026-08-28/29 re-runs, which nothing downstream uses. The
backbone that M12 actually selected — the 5-block/width-48 CNN in
`Asif's/M2/M2_best_model.pth`, which M13, M15, M17, M29 and the concept engine all build
on — has still **never been evaluated on a corrected split**. Re-deciding *that* requires
evaluating the original checkpoints, which is the same script with different paths.

## A caution both candidates share

Sensitivity is ~0.20 for both (0.2005 and 0.198). Both models
detect roughly one abnormal cycle in five, and M3's lead comes almost entirely from
specificity (0.7435 → 0.829). "Which backbone is better" is a narrow
question when neither detects the abnormal class.

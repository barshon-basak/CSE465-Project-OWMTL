\begin{abstract}
Respiratory sound classification on the ICBHI 2017 database has become a saturated
benchmark, with well over a hundred published systems reporting a single headline
score. This paper asks a prior question: how much of that score is a property of the
model, and how much is a property of how it was measured. We audit our own
multi-model respiratory sound pipeline and report four measurement faults, each
recovered from committed confusion matrices rather than asserted, and we measure what
each is worth rather than assuming they matter equally. First, a
macro-averaged variant of the challenge score inflated our results by an average of
nine and a half points across twenty committed runs, and by twenty-two points at worst.
Second, a silently substituted patient-identifier rule produced a test partition of
eleven patients and seven percent of the respiratory cycles while being recorded as
the official sixty-forty split; re-run unchanged on the true split, the same
architectures lose eight to fourteen points. Third, the published split assigns
recordings rather than patients, so two patients appear on both sides of it and it is
not patient independent as it is routinely described---though repairing that leakage
moves our best model by four tenths of a point, well inside its own confidence
interval, so we report it as a validity fault and explicitly not as a source of
inflation. Fourth, the same split file names a recording whose device suffix does not
match the released audio, so a pipeline joining the two on the file stem drops it
silently. Two of the four change the number materially and two do not, and saying which
is which is part of the contribution. We then report a pre-registered
validity gate for physics-derived acoustic concepts, run twice and failed twice, and
read that failure against published human performance on the same labels, where seven
senior physicians reach a score below fifty percent and twelve physicians agree on
detailed adventitious sound descriptions only poorly. Two probes then locate that failure. A foundation model never exposed to
respiratory audio already encodes thirteen of our fourteen acoustic concepts, so the
information is present; adapting that model to the benchmark with a third of a percent
of its parameters degrades thirteen of the fourteen. A supervised probe on the same
unadapted representation, fitted on the training patients alone, then reaches an area
under the curve of 0.71 for crackles and 0.76 for wheezes on the identical test cycles
the gate failed on, above the threshold that gate had set. The labels are therefore
learnable, and we retract---against a reading pre-registered before the probe was
run---the claim that label reliability bounds concept-level detection. Our detectors
were weak, and we say so. What survives is sharper: on the same cycles a physician
agrees with these labels at $\kappa=0.035$ for crackles, reproducing the twenty-three
percent sensitivity of the published seven-physician study, so machines reproduce this
reference standard substantially better than trained listeners agree with it. We
further report a non-replication of our own: an interpretability cost we had recorded
from a single random initialisation reverses sign across five, and we withdraw it. We
release the corrected split loader, a canonical score format with paired significance
tests, and the audit tool that detects all four faults. Evaluated on the published
partition our best model reaches 0.5641, level with the strongest convolutional systems
in the literature; its sensitivity matches systems six points ahead of it on the
composite score, and the whole of its deficit is specificity. The contribution is a
measurement rather than a mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Auscultation remains the first-line examination for respiratory disease, and it is
also one of the least reproducible. The acoustic events that clinicians listen
for---crackles and wheezes---are short, low-energy, and easily masked by heart
sounds, ambient noise, and stethoscope handling. Automating their detection is
therefore an attractive target, and the ICBHI 2017 Respiratory Sound Database has
become the field's default proving ground for it. We chose this problem because the
benchmark is mature enough that a new architecture is unlikely to move it far, which
makes it a good place to ask a different kind of question: whether the numbers the
field reports on it mean what they are taken to mean.

Recent work on ICBHI is dominated by architectural refinement. Suma
\emph{et al.}~\cite{suma2025mtl} train a shared backbone with separate lung-sound and
lung-disease heads across 2D-CNN, ResNet50, MobileNet and DenseNet variants,
reporting 74\% sound-event and 91\% disease accuracy, and represent the closed-set
multi-task baseline for this corpus. ADFF-Net~\cite{adffnet2025} pushes the
transformer branch with an attention-based dual-stream fusion of mel-filterbank and
mel-spectrogram representations. A 2025 study in \emph{Biomedical Signal Processing
and Control}~\cite{bspc2025fusion} fuses time-domain and two-dimensional spectral
features through co-attention and reports a further gain on the same four-class task.
A technique review of the corpus~\cite{electronics2025review} surveys well over a
hundred publications built on it, which is the clearest available evidence that the
benchmark is crowded rather than unsolved.

A parallel line of work asks what a model should do when the input does not belong to
any trained class. Cho and Lee~\cite{cho2025openset} give the only open-set
recognition treatment of ICBHI respiratory audio we are aware of, using
prototype-distance rejection. Yang \emph{et al.}~\cite{yang2024ood} supply the
taxonomy that unifies out-of-distribution detection, open-set recognition and novelty
detection, and Kejriwal \emph{et al.}~\cite{kejriwal2024owl} formalise the
detect--characterise--adapt cycle of open-world learning. Interpretability has
arrived alongside: a 2025 \emph{Scientific Reports} study~\cite{scirep2025cnnrnn}
applies Grad-CAM, LIME and SHAP to a CNN--RNN fusion trained on ICBHI and Coswara.
Concept-based interpretability, in which a model is forced to route its prediction
through named human-meaningful quantities~\cite{koh2020cbm}, is the natural endpoint
of that trend, and it is the direction this project originally set out to take.

Three studies sit uneasily beside all of the above. Tzeng
\emph{et al.}~\cite{tzeng2025jmir} had seven senior physicians blindly annotate a
random quarter of the ICBHI test set; on clean audio they reached a 47.77\% ICBHI
score at 23.23\% sensitivity, missing roughly three quarters of the cycles the
database marks abnormal, at a mean self-rated confidence of 2.88 out of 5.
Melbye \emph{et al.}~\cite{melbye2016kappa} had twelve physicians classify lung
sounds from audiovisual recordings and found agreement on \emph{detailed}
adventitious-sound descriptions---the fine-versus-coarse crackle distinction among
them---at $\kappa < 0.40$, rising to $\kappa = 0.62$ and $\kappa = 0.59$ only when
categories were collapsed to ``crackles'' and ``wheezes''. Huang
\emph{et al.}~\cite{huang2024crackles} compared physicians against a trained model on a
separate clinical breath-sound archive and concluded that crackles are unreliable as a
physical finding for \emph{both}, a weakness the two share rather than one the model
introduces.

\textbf{The gap.} The architectural literature validates detectors \emph{against}
ICBHI labels without characterising what those labels can support, and it reports a
single scalar score whose definition, whose test partition, and whose patient
independence are rarely stated precisely enough to reproduce. The reliability
literature measures the labels but is not connected to the benchmark numbers. Nobody,
to our knowledge, has asked what the reference standard on this corpus can actually
be used to validate, or has published the measurement faults that arise when it is
used carelessly.

This paper closes that gap from the inside, by auditing our own pipeline rather than
anyone else's. Our contributions are four, and each is evidenced by an artefact
committed in the project repository.

First, we document \emph{three evaluation faults} found in our own work and supply
corrected baselines for each. The score we had been reporting was
$(\text{recall}_{\text{macro}} + \text{specificity}_{\text{macro}})/2$ rather than
the ICBHI challenge metric, which inflated fifteen committed runs by 0.1084 on
average and by 0.2176 at worst. The split we had recorded as the official 60/40
partition was in fact a silent patient-identifier fallback yielding eleven test
patients and 7.1\% of the cycles. The genuine official split assigns recordings, not
patients, so two patients appear on both sides of it.

Second, we report a \emph{pre-registered concept-validity gate} that was run twice and
failed twice. Nine physics-derived acoustic concepts are computed per respiratory
cycle by digital signal processing alone---no model, no training, no labels---of
which exactly two can be validated against ICBHI. Both fell far short of the gate on
both runs. We stopped after two runs by design, because the test partition had then
been read twice and a third revision would have been fitting it.

Third, we place that failure against the \emph{human reliability ceiling} on the same
labels, and argue that concept-level validation on this corpus is bounded by label
reliability in a way the literature does not report.

Fourth, we release the \emph{tooling}: a corrected split loader that refuses to fall
back silently, a canonical raw-score format with paired DeLong, McNemar and bootstrap
tests, and a repository audit tool that detects all three fault classes.

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:proposed}
describes the proposed system: the dataset, the preprocessing pipeline, the applied
models, and the corrected evaluation protocol that governs all of them.
Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the results and their discussion, including the
metric and split audits, the model comparison, the augmentation study, the concept
validity gate, the interpretability analysis, the ablation study, and a comparison
against existing work. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes and identifies future
work.

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{subsec:dataset}

We use the ICBHI 2017 Respiratory Sound Database~\cite{rocha2019icbhi}, which
contains 920 annotated auscultation recordings from 126 patients, acquired with four
different stethoscope models at seven chest locations. Each recording is annotated
into respiratory cycles, and each cycle carries two binary flags---crackle present
and wheeze present---which combine into the four-class sound-event task used
throughout this paper: Normal, Crackle, Wheeze, and Both. The corpus contains 6{,}898
annotated cycles.

The database ships an official train/test list, and its provenance turns out to
matter more than its contents. Table~\ref{tab:splits} states the three partitions
that appear in this work. The published list assigns \emph{recordings}, not patients,
and two patients---156 and 218---have recordings on both sides of it, so it is not
patient independent despite being universally described as such. Our corrected
partition takes the published list and moves every recording of a leaking patient
into training, which is the conservative direction: the test set is then guaranteed
to contain no patient the model saw during training. The correction costs 12 of 381
test recordings, or 3.1\%, and moves the ratio from 58.6/41.4 to 59.9/40.1, closer to
the nominal 60/40. The third partition is not a design at all but a fault, described
in Section~\ref{subsec:split_audit}.

\subsection{Dataset Preprocessing}
\label{subsec:preprocessing}

All models consume the same log-mel representation, fixed once so that backbones
remain comparable. Audio is loaded and resampled to $f_s = 16$~kHz mono. Cycles are
cut from the annotation files' start and end times; the cycle is the unit of
classification, while the \emph{patient} is the unit of splitting. Each cycle is
standardised to $T = 8.0$~s by cyclic repetition when shorter and centre-cropping
when longer. Cyclic repetition is a deliberate choice over zero-padding, because
silence changes the temporal statistics that crackle-rate features depend on; it is
one of the rows of the preprocessing ablation in Section~\ref{subsec:ablation}.

The standardised waveform $x[n]$ is transformed to a mel spectrogram with a
1024-point FFT, a 400-sample (25~ms) Hann window, a 160-sample (10~ms) hop, and $M =
128$ mel filters spanning 50--2000~Hz, which covers the diagnostic band for crackles
and wheezes while rejecting most low-frequency handling noise. Writing $X[m,k]$ for
the short-time Fourier transform and $H_j[k]$ for the $j$-th mel filter, the log-mel
representation is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:logmel}
S[j,m] = 10 \log_{10}\!\left( \sum_{k} H_j[k]\,\bigl| X[m,k] \bigr|^{2} + \epsilon \right),
\end{equation}

\noindent with $\epsilon$ a small constant guarding the logarithm. Each spectrogram is
then standardised to zero mean and unit variance,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:standardize}
\tilde{S}[j,m] = \frac{S[j,m] - \mu_S}{\sigma_S + \epsilon},
\end{equation}

\noindent and, for the ImageNet-initialised backbones, replicated across three
channels and re-normalised with the ImageNet channel statistics.

Class imbalance is handled in the loss rather than by resampling, so that the
effective training distribution is identical across backbones. With $C = 4$ classes,
$N_c$ training cycles in class $c$, and $N = \sum_c N_c$, the weights and objective
are

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weights}
w_c = \frac{N}{C \, N_c},
\qquad
\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B} w_{y_i} \log \hat{p}_{i,y_i},
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\hat{p}_{i,y_i}$ is the predicted probability of the true class of
cycle $i$ in a batch of size $B$. Every model in Table~\ref{tab:baselines} uses
(\ref{eq:weights}) unchanged, which is what makes the backbone comparison fair.

Augmentation is applied to the training partition only, never to validation or test.
The augmented runs use SpecAugment---two frequency masks of at most 24 of the 128 mel
bins and two time masks of at most 80 of the 801 frames---applied to
$\tilde{S}$ at load time, with the clean and augmented runs sharing seed 42 so that
the difference between them isolates the augmentation.

Three stages that appear in the project's written protocol are absent from the model
described above: an explicit band-pass filter, spectral-gating denoising, and per-cycle
amplitude normalisation. The mel filterbank's 50--2000\,Hz limits bound the band
implicitly, so the first is partially subsumed; the other two were simply not performed.
They have since been implemented and are held \emph{off} in this configuration, because
switching one on would move the baseline every ablation delta is measured against. Rather
than leave them as a gap, Section~\ref{subsec:ablation} reports each as an addition to
this pipeline and states what it buys---which for two of the three is negative.

\subsection{Applied Models}
\label{subsec:models}

The model suite spans four families, assigned one primary model per group member.

\emph{Trained-from-scratch convolutional baselines.} A four-block 2D convolutional
network with 32 base channels and 0.3 dropout (421{,}732 parameters) establishes the
floor. It is included because a pre-trained backbone that cannot beat it is not
earning its transfer.

\emph{Pre-trained convolutional backbones.} MobileNetV2 and DenseNet121, both
ImageNet-initialised, fully fine-tuned, with the single-channel log-mel replicated to
three channels. The MobileNetV2 configuration was selected by a two-fold
patient-grouped architecture search over six candidates (MobileNetV3-Small/Large,
MobileNetV2, DenseNet121, and two further variants), scored by mean validation
performance across folds, so the backbone choice is itself a measured decision rather
than a default.

\emph{Transformer backbones.} Three ImageNet-pretrained transformers---ViT-B/16,
Swin-T and DeiT-S---are trained on the corrected partition under settings fixed in
advance so that they stay comparable to each other and to the convolutional rows:
128$\times$801 log-mel resized to the model's native resolution, three-channel
replication with ImageNet normalisation, the class-weighted loss of (\ref{eq:weights}),
seed 42, AdamW at $10^{-4}$ on the backbone and $10^{-3}$ on the head, cosine schedule,
at most 40 epochs, early stopping on validation macro F1, and a clean run followed by a
SpecAugment pair. A fourth, an Audio Spectrogram Transformer, is in progress and is not
reported. We fixed the settings before running and did not tune them afterwards, which
is why the collapse reported in Section~\ref{subsec:split_audit} is reported rather than
repaired.

\emph{Physics-derived concept extractors.} Nine acoustic quantities are computed per
cycle directly from the waveform by digital signal processing, with no learned
parameters: crackle and wheeze scores, crackle fine ratio, crackle rate, wheeze
pitch, a rhonchi score, inspiratory fraction, spectral flatness, and temporal
peak-to-average power ratio. Only two of the nine---crackle presence and wheeze
presence---have a ground truth in ICBHI. Five are proxies for clinical entities the
database does not label, and two are descriptive. That 2/5/2 partition is itself a
result about what this benchmark can validate, and we name the proxies as
physics-derived quantities rather than after the clinical entities they approximate.

Alongside these, the project carries a set of extension models---dynamic multi-task
loss weighting, demographic feature fusion, temporal transformer aggregation over
cycles, curriculum pacing, a physics-informed auxiliary loss, low-rank adapter
fine-tuning, multistage distillation, and an open-set scorer suite---reported in
Section~\ref{subsec:novelty}. These were run under an earlier 70/30 partition and are
therefore reported separately and never ranked against the 60/40 results.

\subsection{Corrected Evaluation Protocol}
\label{subsec:protocol}

The ICBHI 2017 challenge score is a two-term average over \emph{pooled} abnormality.
Let the confusion matrix be $\mathbf{C}$ with rows indexed by truth, class $0$ being
Normal. Specificity is the Normal recall and sensitivity is the pooled recall over
the three abnormal classes:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:official}
Sp = \frac{C_{00}}{\sum_{j} C_{0j}},
\quad
Se = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{3} C_{ii}}{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j} C_{ij}},
\quad
\mathrm{ICBHI} = \frac{Se + Sp}{2}.
\end{equation}

The quantity we had been reporting instead was the macro average of the per-class
recalls and the macro average of the per-class specificities:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:macro}
\mathrm{ICBHI}_{\text{macro}}
= \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c} \mathrm{Rec}_c + \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c} \mathrm{Spec}_c \right).
\end{equation}

Equations (\ref{eq:official}) and (\ref{eq:macro}) differ in a way that is not a
constant offset. In (\ref{eq:macro}) each per-class specificity counts true negatives
drawn from the other three classes, so a rare class such as Wheeze or Both scores
close to 0.95 almost regardless of whether the model detects it at all. The
inflation is therefore largest exactly where the model is worst, and we quantify it
per run as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta}
\Delta = \mathrm{ICBHI}_{\text{macro}} - \mathrm{ICBHI}.
\end{equation}

Three protocol rules follow from this and are enforced repository-wide. Every run
commits its raw confusion matrix, so that any reported score can be recomputed by a
reader; a score without a matrix is treated as unverifiable and is excluded from
every table in this paper. Every run records the provenance of its split by name, and
a notebook that cannot locate the official split file raises rather than falling back.
Every run records parameter count, on-disk size, and wall-clock training time, so
that accuracy claims can be read against their cost.

For the concept extractors, validity is assessed by the area under the receiver
operating characteristic curve against the binary crackle and wheeze flags, with
DeLong 95\% confidence intervals. The gate was pre-registered as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gate}
\text{pass} \iff \mathrm{AUROC} \geq 0.65 \;\wedge\; \mathrm{CI}_{\text{lo}} > 0.5 ,
\end{equation}

\noindent evaluated once per run on the test partition. The explicit AUROC floor in
(\ref{eq:gate}) matters: an earlier version of the rule required only that the
confidence interval exclude chance, and at $n \approx 2{,}600$ that condition is
satisfied by an AUROC of 0.52, which is statistically significant and practically
useless. A gate that cannot fail is not a gate.

Two observations matter more than the mean. The first is that the inflation is

official metric is not merely the conventional one, it is the one that fails loudly.

\subsection{Split Audit}

The single best model has now been run on all three treatments of the split, which
separates two faults that an earlier version of this analysis reported as one.
MobileNetV2 with SpecAugment scores 0.6495 on the fallback partition, 0.5641 on the
published partition verbatim, and 0.5602 on the corrected one. The two differences are
not the same size and do not carry the same weight.

The first, $-0.0854$, is what the silent fallback was worth. It is corroborated by the
tuned scratch CNN, which falls from 0.6138 to 0.4720, and it is the same order as the
one controlled figure in the literature: RespireNet reports 0.562 on the official split
and 0.685 under five-fold cross-validation with the same model, a swing of $0.123$
\cite{respirenet2021}. We still decline to call it exactly one-variable, because the
re-runs also select their checkpoint on the official metric rather than the macro
variant, and a checkpoint chosen on the wrong metric is not the same model; the drop is
reported as split provenance plus metric-correct selection together.

The second, $-0.0039$, is what repairing the benchmark's patient leakage was worth, and
here the comparison \emph{is} exactly one-variable: same architecture, same seed, same
schedule, same class weights, same selection metric, differing only in whether the two
overlapping patients are reassigned to train. It is a fortieth of the first difference,
it sits deep inside the verbatim run's patient-level bootstrap interval of
$[0.5121, 0.6144]$, and the two runs selected different epochs, 26 against 38. We
therefore state plainly that \textbf{correcting the published split's patient leakage does
not measurably change the score at this sample size}. The leakage is a validity fault, not
a source of inflation, and we resist the temptation to present it as the latter. What the
repair does change is the operating point rather than the composite: sensitivity falls
from 0.4562 to 0.4089 while specificity rises from 0.6719 to 0.7115, two shifts of
comparable size that nearly cancel in the average---an illustration of the same case for
reporting $Se$ and $Sp$ separately that Section~\ref{subsec:protocol} makes.

The third fault is a property of the benchmark rather than of our code. The published
split list assigns recordings, so patient 156 contributes 9 training and 8 test
recordings and patient 218 contributes 4 and 4, and 120 of the 2{,}756 published test
cycles---4.4\%---belong to a patient the model may have trained on. Any work describing
the published ICBHI split as patient-independent is, strictly, describing something else.
The same file carries a fourth, smaller defect that surfaced only when we ran against it
verbatim: it lists \texttt{226\_1b1\_Pl\_sc\_Meditron}, while the released audio contains
\texttt{226\_1b1\_Pl\_sc\_LittC2SE}. The device suffix disagrees, so any pipeline that
joins audio to split assignment on the file stem silently drops that recording and its
eleven cycles. It falls on the training side, so it does not affect the comparability of
the verbatim row, but it is the same class of fault as the other three: a provenance
defect that is invisible unless a run is made to assert its own partition rather than
assume it. Our corrected loader reports all modes by name, refuses to call the corrected
one ``official'', reports unmatched stems rather than skipping them silently, and raises
rather than substituting a fallback when the split file is missing.

Having identified the defect, we measured what it is worth, and the answer is not the one
the defect's existence suggests. We trained the same architecture and recipe twice: once on
the published split verbatim, once with the two dual-side patients reassigned to training.
The verbatim run scores 0.5641 and the corrected run 0.5602, but those are different test
sets---2{,}756 cycles against 2{,}636---so the gap is not a measurement of anything. Scored
on the 2{,}636 cycles both runs share, the verbatim-trained model reaches 0.5675 against the
corrected model's 0.5602, a difference of $+0.0073$ whose paired patient-level interval is
$[-0.020, +0.030]$ ($p = 0.562$). The two are indistinguishable.

The direct test is more pointed still. If training on patients 156 and 218 helped the model
on those patients, it should score better on their 120 test cycles than on everyone else's.
It scores \emph{worse}: 0.4875 against 0.5675, and 42.5\% accuracy against 58.7\%. Whatever
those two patients contribute, an unearned advantage is not it; they are simply harder than
average, and with $n = 2$ patients that is a description rather than a result.

We report this because it bounds our own criticism. The published split is not
patient-independent, and describing it as such remains wrong---but on this corpus the defect
does not inflate the headline score, and a reader entitled to ask ``how much does this
actually matter'' deserves the measured answer rather than the implication. Of the three
faults in this section, the metric (Table~\ref{tab:metricaudit}, $+0.0948$ mean) and the
split fallback ($-0.089$ on the best model) change what the numbers mean. This one changes
what they may be called.

\subsection{Do the Models Represent the Concepts at All?}
\label{subsec:probe}

Section~\ref{subsec:conceptgate} shows that our hand-built detectors do not track the
ICBHI cycle labels. That leaves two very different explanations, and the gate cannot
separate them. Either the acoustic information is not present in a form a model can
use---a representational failure---or it is present, and the labels do not follow it---a
referential one. A linear probe distinguishes them directly: if a frozen network's
embedding linearly predicts a concept, the network represents that concept, whatever
the labels say.

We probe four representations for all fourteen concepts. Two are foundation models: an
Audio Spectrogram Transformer~\cite{gong2021ast} pre-trained on
AudioSet~\cite{gemmeke2017audioset} and never exposed to respiratory audio, and the
same model after low-rank adaptation~\cite{hu2022lora} to the four-class ICBHI task
($r=8$ on the query and value projections of all twelve blocks, $294{,}912$ trainable
parameters, 0.34\% of the total). The third is our own M2 CNN, trained from scratch on
this task. The fourth is a random projection of the same dimensionality, which is the
control that makes the others readable: a linear probe on a 768-dimensional embedding
with roughly a hundred patients will appear to work on noise unless it is checked.
Continuous concepts are scored by ridge $R^2$ and binary ones by AUROC, always under
patient-grouped cross-validation, with bootstrap intervals and a within-patient
permutation null.

\midrule

The first row settles the question. A model that has never heard a stethoscope
recording already encodes thirteen of the fourteen concepts, several strongly---wheeze
duration ratio at $R^2=0.777$, spectral flatness at $0.759$, rhonchi presence at
$0.648$---while the random control recovers nothing at all. The acoustic structure our
detectors were built to measure is present and linearly accessible. The gate failure in
Section~\ref{subsec:conceptgate} is therefore not a representational failure.
Section~\ref{subsec:ceiling} closes the remaining alternative by showing that the labels
are reachable too, which leaves the detectors themselves.

The second row is the more uncomfortable result. Adapting 0.34\% of the parameters to
the ICBHI task \emph{reduces} concept recoverability on thirteen of the fourteen
concepts, with a mean change of $-0.0907$ in $R^2$. Task adaptation, at the smallest
scale we could apply it, systematically discards clinically named structure that the
pre-trained model had already learned. The concept degraded most is rhonchi presence,
at $-0.219$; Section~\ref{subsec:bottleneck} independently identifies rhonchi presence
as the only one of the fourteen carrying appreciable diagnostic information, from a
different estimator on different data. Two analyses converge on the same concept from
opposite directions, and the direction of travel is that fitting this benchmark costs
the model exactly the structure a clinician would name.

Fine crackle ratio is the one concept no representation recovers ($R^2 \approx 0$
everywhere). It was already the concept that never functioned on real audio, and it
targets the fine-versus-coarse distinction on which physicians themselves agree at
$\kappa<0.40$. Three independent lines of evidence now say the same thing about it.

\subsection{What These Labels Actually Support}
\label{subsec:ceiling}

The previous subsection established that the acoustic information is present in a
learned representation. It did not establish that the \emph{labels} can be reached from
it. That is the question the gate could not answer about itself, and answering it
decides whether our gate failure is a statement about our detectors or about the
benchmark. We registered the readings before running the experiment.

A supervised probe is the right instrument because it is strictly easier than the task
the gate set: it is allowed to see the labels. If a model given that advantage still
cannot clear 0.65, the threshold is unreachable here and the gate failure is a property
of the reference standard. If it clears it comfortably, the reference standard is not
the binding constraint and our detectors simply were not good enough. We fit a
standardised logistic probe on the 79 training patients and score it on the same 2{,}636
test cycles from the same 47 test patients that the gate was scored on, with intervals
bootstrapped over patients. Three feature spaces are compared: the frozen AudioSet AST
embedding of Section~\ref{subsec:probe}, which has never seen a respiratory corpus and
is therefore the arm no circularity can be alleged against; our own M2 encoder, which
was trained on these very labels and is reported as the optimistic bound it is; and our
fourteen physics concepts used as a vector rather than as the single score the gate
thresholded.

The pre-registered ``no ceiling'' branch fires, and we report it as such. A network that
has never heard a stethoscope recording supports crackle detection at 0.71 and wheeze
detection at 0.76 on the exact cycles our extractors failed on. These labels are
learnable well above the threshold the gate set. We therefore retract the reading that
this paper's earlier drafts, and our own decision record, had placed on the gate
failure: label reliability is \emph{not} the binding constraint on concept-level
detection here. Our detectors were weak. Writing the ``no ceiling'' branch down in
advance is what makes that concession evidence rather than a change of story.

A second result falls out of the same table, and it is a criticism of our own
experimental design rather than of the data. The gate thresholded a single hand-built
scalar. The same fourteen concepts, used as a vector, reach 0.6608 on crackles---above
the threshold the scalar failed. Part of what the gate measured was the scalar
summarisation, not the concept vocabulary. A validity gate for a concept suite should
therefore be posed over the suite, not over one score at a time, and we report ours as
an example of the difference that choice makes.

supports. A frozen AudioSet model reaches 0.76 on the wheeze labels having never heard
a stethoscope, while a self-consistent physician agrees with those same labels at
$\kappa = 0.035$ on crackles. \emph{Machines reproduce this reference standard
substantially better than trained listeners agree with it.} That is not a ceiling
argument and it is not a claim that the labels are wrong; it says that whatever ICBHI's
cycle annotations encode is more accessible to a spectrogram model than it is
reproducible by ear. For a benchmark whose labels are routinely described as a clinical
reference, that gap is worth reporting on its own.

One concept remains bounded by human agreement, and only one. Our fine crackle ratio
targeted the fine-versus-coarse distinction, on which twelve physicians agree at
$\kappa < 0.40$; no representation in Section~\ref{subsec:probe} recovers it, and it is
constant across the clinician-labelled subset, so it cannot be validated here by any
route. Collapsing to presence-only categories raises human agreement to
$\kappa \approx 0.6$, and it is the presence-only concepts that both the probe and
Table~\ref{tab:ceiling} recover well.

We scope the claim deliberately. We are not claiming that ICBHI's labels are wrong, that
the benchmark should be abandoned, or that concept-based methods cannot work for
auscultation---and we are no longer claiming, as an earlier draft did, that label
reliability bounds concept-level detection. We claim that these labels are learnable by
a model well above the threshold our gate set, that a self-consistent clinician
nonetheless reproduces them poorly, and that our own physics-derived detectors did not
reach what the labels support. We release the protocol, the tooling and the 132
fine-grained annotated cycles that let others check each part.

The listening study is a single rater, so no inter-rater agreement is computable from
it; the published $n=7$ and $n=12$ studies carry the population claim, ours adds the
first fine/coarse crackle reference on this corpus, and the bound on what the labels
support comes from Table~\ref{tab:ceiling} rather than from any rater count.

\subsection{The Concept Bottleneck and a Non-Replication}
\label{subsec:bottleneck}

A concept bottleneck~\cite{koh2020cbm} forces the diagnosis through the interpretable
concept vector, and the accuracy it gives up is the usual price of that
interpretability. We evaluate four variants at patient level on the corrected
partition: \emph{independent}, in which the disease is predicted from the DSP concepts
alone; \emph{sequential}, in which a learned predictor maps encoder features to
concepts and the disease is read from those; \emph{leaky}, which sees concepts and
encoder features together and can therefore bypass the bottleneck; and \emph{opaque},
which ignores concepts entirely and upper-bounds the trade-off.

Our own first run of this comparison used a single random initialisation and produced a
clean monotone story: macro-F1 fell from 0.5973 (opaque) through 0.5457 (leaky) and
0.4441 (sequential), and we recorded an interpretability cost of 0.2326 in accuracy and
0.1432 in macro-F1. Table~\ref{tab:bottleneck} repeats the experiment across five
seeds.

The ordering inverts. Under five seeds the strict bottleneck attains the \emph{highest}

predictions gives $p=1.0000$. The interpretability cost we previously recorded does not
survive the addition of four more initialisations, and we withdraw it: at 43 test
patients there is no measurable cost to routing this diagnosis through the concepts, in
either direction.

We report this at length because it is the same class of error as the metric and split
faults in Sections~\ref{subsec:metric_audit} and~\ref{subsec:split_audit}---a number
taken at a precision the evidence does not support---and because it was our own, found
only by re-running an experiment we had already recorded as finished. A single seed on
43 patients is not an ablation.

The shuffled-concept control is the check the first run omitted, and it is the one
positive finding here. Replacing each patient's concept vector with another patient's
costs 0.2291 macro-F1 (95\% CI $[0.004, 0.441]$, $p=0.045$), so the concept values do
carry patient-specific information beyond their marginal distribution. On accuracy the
same comparison is null ($+0.0930$, CI $[-0.093, 0.279]$, $p=0.389$), because accuracy
on this three-class task is dominated by COPD, which is 64 of 104 patients. Both
numbers belong in the record; either alone misleads.

\subsection{Covariate Shift: Do Adult Acoustic Priors Transfer to Children?}
\label{subsec:pediatric}

The device axis of this corpus is unusable for a shift study---only three of 126
patients span more than one stethoscope---so the pediatric population is the single
covariate stress available to us. We compare the fourteen concepts, computed by the
same extractors without modification, between ICBHI and SPRSound~\cite{zhang2022sprsound}
(6{,}656 annotated events from 243 children, ages 0.2--16.2 years, median 4.4).
SPRSound events are cut from their annotated start and end times so that the acoustic
unit matches ICBHI's annotated cycle; fixed-length windowing would compare one breath
against several and measure segmentation rather than physiology.

A distance between two corpora does not explain itself, so we state directional
predictions on named concepts in advance. Two mechanisms generate them. Frequency
scaling: a child's airway is shorter and narrower, so resonant frequency rises and
spectral quantities shift upward. Allometric respiratory rate: smaller bodies breathe
faster, so cycles shorten and events occupy a larger fraction of them. Six concepts
follow from neither and are named as controls.

Two caveats govern how the result may be read. First, four predictions were fixed
before any pediatric audio was opened; four more were added afterwards, because a
four-item one-sided binomial cannot fall below $p=0.0625$ and therefore could not have
supported the hypothesis whatever the data showed. The extension bought the test power,
but it is an extension and not a pre-registration, and we report the two sets
separately. Second, the cross-corpus comparison confounds airway size with corpus,
stethoscope, protocol and annotator. We therefore lead with a within-cohort analysis
that holds all four fixed: the correlation of each concept with age \emph{inside}
SPRSound. If the mechanism is real it must appear there too.

Five of seven estimable concepts move as predicted with intervals excluding zero, and
the three frequency quantities---dominant frequency, wheeze dominant frequency and the
low/high ratio---are the strongest and most consistent of them. That is the pattern
inverse frequency scaling predicts, in a comparison where corpus, device and protocol
are constant.

We nonetheless decline to call the mechanism established. Two concepts move
\emph{against} prediction with intervals excluding zero, and the sign test over all
predictions does not reach significance in either arm ($5/7$, $p=0.227$ within cohort;
$5/8$, $p=0.363$ cross-corpus). Restricted to the four genuinely pre-registered
predictions the test is $3/4$ in both arms, which is the best result its 0.0625 floor
permits and still not significant. The honest summary is that the frequency concepts
behave as the physics predicts and the crackle-morphology concepts do not, and that a
mechanism claim needs either more named predictions or a second pediatric corpus. We
report the concept-space maximum mean discrepancy alongside, at $0.4805$ (CI
$[0.457, 0.502]$), which is a shift of a size the earlier embedding-space figure of
0.4236 was reported without any interval at all.

This subsection also records a methodological point worth more than the result. Our
first version of this analysis judged the mechanism solely by the four-prediction sign
test and duly printed ``not supported''---a verdict that was determined by the design
rather than the data, since no outcome could have produced $p<0.05$. A test that cannot
pass is not evidence of absence.

\subsection{Interpretability Analysis}
\label{subsec:xai}

We apply Grad-CAM over the final convolutional block of the best model and occlusion
sensitivity as a model-agnostic cross-check, on 400 test cycles under the corrected
partition. Fig.~\ref{fig:xai} shows one correctly classified example of each class
alongside a misclassification.

The analysis we had planned was a pointing game: ICBHI annotates crackle and wheeze
events, so we intended to measure whether the attribution mass falls inside the
annotated event window. It is not implementable on this corpus, and the reason is worth
recording. ICBHI annotates one row per respiratory \emph{cycle}---start, end, and
whether that cycle contains a crackle or a wheeze---not where inside the cycle the event
occurs. Our pipeline crops to exactly that window, so the annotated window \emph{is} the
model input and the hit rate is 100\% by construction. There is no finer temporal
reference in the corpus to score against, which is the same absence of a fine-grained
reference standard that Section~\ref{subsec:ceiling} reports from the other direction.

We substitute two measurements that the annotations do support. The first is band
pointing: what fraction of the attribution mass falls in the 100--1000\,Hz range where
wheezes live. If the model were listening for wheezes where a clinician would, cycles
labelled wheeze should draw \emph{more} mass into that band. They draw less---0.714
against 0.767 for cycles without a wheeze, a difference of $-0.053$ with a
patient-bootstrap interval of $[-0.079, -0.026]$ and $p = 0.001$, against a uniform
baseline of 0.555. The second is a consistency check that needs no annotation at all.
Cycles shorter than 8\,s are wrap-padded, so the same audio appears two or three times
in one input; a model keying on the sound should attribute identically to each
repetition. Across 361 evaluable cycles the mean correlation between repetitions is
$0.098$ (median $0.180$), and only 32\% exceed $0.5$. The model is partly keying on
position within the input window rather than on the sound in it.

Two caveats bound this. MobileNetV2 downsamples by 32, so the final block is a
$4 \times 26$ grid---roughly 500\,Hz by 0.31\,s per cell---and every map in
Fig.~\ref{fig:xai} is that grid bilinearly upsampled: the apparent detail is
interpolation, not measurement. And this is evidence about \emph{where the model looks},
not clinical validation. Attributions aligned to a reference standard that a
self-consistent clinician reproduces at $\kappa = 0.035$ could not certify clinical
reasoning even if they had pointed the other way.

\subsection{Best Model and Ablation Study}
\label{subsec:ablation}

We define the best model once, in advance, to avoid selecting it after the fact:
\emph{the highest official ICBHI score on the corrected 60/40 partition, among runs
that commit a raw confusion matrix}. Under that rule the reference model is M22-v2,
MobileNetV2 with SpecAugment on the corrected partition, at 0.5602. It is not the
largest number the project has produced, and that is the point of fixing the rule in
advance: M22's 0.6495 is on the fallback partition and M35's 0.6864 is on the 70/30
partition, so neither can be ranked against it. The three transformers are also ranked
by the same rule and all fall below it, with the best of them, Swin-T, at 0.5304 for
twelve times the parameters.

One row of the ablation that follows complicates this, and we report the complication
rather than quietly resolving it. Row P3---M22-v2 with per-cycle peak amplitude
normalisation added---scores 0.5764 on the same partition and commits a confusion matrix,
so the letter of the rule selects it. Its advantage over M22-v2 is $+0.0162$ with a
patient-level paired interval of $[-0.017, +0.046]$ and $p = 0.367$: not distinguishable
from the reference at the unit of analysis this paper argues is the correct one. We
therefore continue to use M22-v2 as the reference model for the diagnostics below and for
Table~\ref{tab:comparison}, on the grounds that a selection rule should not be settled by
a difference the data cannot support, and we record P3 as the one preprocessing stage
worth adopting on the evidence available, pending a multi-seed confirmation this project
has not run. Naming the tie-break after seeing the number is a deviation from the
discipline of fixing the rule in advance, and we flag it as one rather than presenting
the outcome as though the rule had produced it.

Fig.~\ref{fig:cm} and Fig.~\ref{fig:curves} give the required diagnostics for that
model, both drawn from its committed confusion matrix and per-epoch history so that no
number in the figures can drift from the table. The confusion matrix shows where the
imbalance bites: Normal is recovered at 0.712 and Crackle at 0.519, while Wheeze reaches
0.295 and Both---86 test cycles---reaches 0.116, with most of the mass of both minority
classes falling into Normal and Crackle.

The training curves record a disagreement worth stating rather than smoothing. Validation
loss reaches its minimum at epoch 5 and rises thereafter, the ordinary overfitting
signature, but the checkpoint is selected at epoch 38 because selection is on the
official ICBHI score, as the protocol requires. The two criteria disagree by 33 epochs:
the model keeps improving on the challenge metric long after it has stopped improving on
the loss it is trained with. This is a direct consequence of the metric correction of
Section~\ref{subsec:metric_audit}---cross-entropy on a corpus that is 59\% Normal and the
balanced $(Se+Sp)/2$ do not optimise the same thing---and it is why every corrected re-run
here selects on the reported metric rather than on validation loss.

Two things in this table are worth more than the ordering. The first is that of the
three preprocessing stages our own protocol specified and our pipeline never implemented,
only one helps: per-cycle amplitude normalisation gains $+0.0162$, while an explicit
band-pass costs $-0.0089$ and spectral-gating denoising costs $-0.0355$. The band-pass
result is the least surprising --- the mel filterbank's 50--2000\,Hz limits already bound
the band, so an explicit Butterworth stage mostly re-does work the front end had done ---
but the denoising result is a caution worth reporting: the stage a reviewer is most likely
to ask for is the one that hurt most.

The second is a disagreement between two ways of testing the same ten rows, and it is
the more useful finding. We tested every row against A0 twice: with McNemar over the
2{,}636 paired cycle predictions, and with a paired bootstrap over the 47 test patients,
resampling patients and rescoring both models on each resample.

Scored per cycle, seven of the ten rows are significant at $\alpha = 0.05$. Scored per
patient, \emph{one} is: A4, freezing the backbone, at $-0.1008$ with an interval of
$[-0.154, -0.052]$. Every other interval spans zero, including rows whose cycle-level
$p$-values are vanishingly small --- A2 and A6 both return $p < 10^{-15}$ per cycle and
$[-0.130, +0.003]$ and $[-0.092, +0.009]$ per patient. Six of the ten rows flip verdict
between the two units.

The reason is not subtle, which is what makes it worth reporting: cycles within a patient
are not independent, so a cycle-level test answers a question about 2{,}636 correlated
draws and reports the answer as though they were 2{,}636 independent ones. The effective
sample size of this ablation is 47, not 2{,}636. An ablation table reporting cycle-level
significance would have licensed six conclusions this data does not support, and it would
have looked more rigorous than the table that does not.

That has a direct consequence for this subsection's own headline. P3's 0.5764 is the
highest score in the table, above the A0 baseline the best-model rule selected. We do
\emph{not} promote it. By the score P3 leads; by the paired test the two are
indistinguishable on 47 patients, and a single un-replicated row that is not shown to beat
its own baseline is a lead, not a model. Amplitude normalisation is worth adopting and
re-running properly. It is not worth a re-declaration on this evidence.

The one conclusion the ablation does support is therefore A4's: fine-tuning the backbone
rather than freezing it is worth $0.10$ of official score, and it is the only component
whose contribution this test set is large enough to establish.

Read on the partition the literature actually uses, our best model reaches 0.5641. It
places above RespireNet, the strongest ImageNet-pretrained convolutional system in
Table~\ref{tab:comparison}, and below every audio-pretrained one, 8.4 points short of the
strongest system in the table. The composite score, however, conceals the more informative
comparison. Our sensitivity of 0.456 is not behind the field at all: it exceeds RespireNet
at 0.401, supervised contrastive pretraining at 0.392 and Patch-Mix at 0.431, and is level
with BTS at 0.457, trailing only the frozen M2D probe at 0.466 and PAFA at 0.476. The
deficit is specificity, 0.672 against a field spanning 0.722 to 0.821. We are not uniformly
weaker than the published systems; we occupy a different operating point, detecting
abnormal cycles at the field's rate while over-calling them on normal ones---the direction
a class-weighted loss and SpecAugment push a model, and the direction a screening
application would prefer, though the challenge metric does not reward it. This is the
concrete payoff of the reporting discipline of Section~\ref{subsec:protocol}: under the
composite alone our system reads simply as worse, and the distinction disappears.

The honest reading of the rest of the table is that our numbers do not lead it, and that
is the point. Our uncorrected numbers would have led it, and they were wrong twice over. A
system reporting 0.7227 on a 7.1\% test partition under a non-standard metric is not state
of the art; it is an unaudited measurement, and it would have been compared against a
literature evaluated on 2{,}756 cycles under (\ref{eq:official}).

\subsection{What the Gap Is Made Of}
\label{subsec:pretraining}

An eight-point deficit invites the assumption that it is a matter of scale or
tuning. On this benchmark it is neither. Five measurements, four of them ours, converge
on a single cause, and it is the pretraining corpus rather than the architecture.

The first is visible in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}, and the ordering there is not mixed:
every published system that outscores ours is built on an audio-domain-pretrained
backbone---AudioSet, CLAP or BEATs---and the only one below ours is the sole
ImageNet-pretrained convolutional entry. The transition in the literature is usually narrated as the arrival of
transformers, but the two changes are confounded in every paper that reports it: the
transformers that appear on this benchmark are also the first models pretrained on
audio.

The second separates them. Niizumi \emph{et al.}~\cite{frozenfm2025} evaluate
AudioSet-pretrained encoders on the official partition with the encoder \emph{frozen} and
a single linear layer trained on top, and reach 0.5938. That is above every model in this
paper, all of which were fully fine-tuned, and it is achieved without adapting the
representation to respiratory audio at all. The same work reports that a
respiratory-specific foundation model pretrained on 140{,}000 respiratory sounds
underperforms general models pretrained on two million diverse ones, which places the
weight on corpus scale and diversity rather than domain match.

The third is ours and is the control the literature lacks. Because our transformers are
ImageNet-pretrained rather than AudioSet-pretrained, Table~\ref{tab:baselines} isolates
architecture from pretraining corpus: Swin-T at 27.5M parameters reaches 0.5304, DeiT-S at
21.7M reaches 0.5149, and ViT-B/16 at 85.8M collapses to the majority class, while a
2.2M-parameter MobileNetV2 reaches 0.5602 on the same partition under the same recipe.
Twelve to thirty-nine times the parameters, and every one of them worse. Transformer
capacity without an audio prior buys nothing here, and the ViT-B collapse is the expected
endpoint of that trend rather than an anomaly: 85.8M parameters fitted to 4{,}251 training
cycles with no in-domain initialisation.

The fourth is the ablation. Removing ImageNet initialisation costs 0.0603, the largest
single-component effect in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} other than removing fine-tuning
altogether. Pretraining is already the dominant term when the corpus is merely images.

The fifth is Section~\ref{subsec:ceiling}. A frozen AudioSet representation that has never
heard a stethoscope recovers the crackle and wheeze labels at 0.7115 and 0.7621 on the
same cycles our physics-derived detectors reached 0.5580 and 0.5729 on. That result is the
concept-level instance of the same finding, and read beside \cite{frozenfm2025} it is a
replication rather than a curiosity.

The practical reading is that the most valuable single change available to this system is
not a larger model, a longer schedule or a better augmentation, but an encoder pretrained
on general audio. That is a cheap conclusion to state and an expensive one to have had to
establish, and it is worth stating precisely because the confound it resolves is present
throughout the benchmark's published history.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Seven limitations bound the claims above. The split effect is now measured rather than
estimated for one model, which was run on all three treatments of the partition, but not
for the suite: the fallback comparison rests on two architectures and remains confounded
with metric-correct checkpoint selection. Only the leakage-repair difference, $-0.0039$,
is a clean one-variable measurement, and being a null at one seed it bounds the effect
loosely rather than establishing its absence. The concept extractors are one
implementation and a weak one, which Section~\ref{subsec:ceiling} establishes rather
than concedes: a supervised probe clears the gate threshold on the same cycles, so the
gap is ours. What that probe does not show is that a \emph{hand-built} detector can
reach 0.65 on these labels---a learned probe and a physics-derived score are not the
same object---so the practical ceiling for interpretable, non-learned concept detection
on this corpus remains open. Our probe is also linear and single-recipe; a different
head could move the number in either direction. The human side rests on two published
studies plus our single-clinician study, against the $n=7$ and $n=12$ of the literature,
and a single rater cannot yield inter-rater agreement.

The bottleneck comparison of Section~\ref{subsec:bottleneck} is run on 43 test patients,
where the intervals are wide enough that the ordering between adjacent variants carries
little information; the claim we make from it is a negative one, that no
interpretability cost is measurable, and even that is a statement about this sample
size. The adaptation result of Section~\ref{subsec:probe} uses one adaptation recipe at
one rank for five epochs, so it shows that concept encoding degrades under this
adaptation, not that degradation scales with adaptation strength. The pediatric analysis
mixes four pre-registered predictions with four added afterwards and reaches
significance under neither, so it is reported as a pattern consistent with frequency
scaling rather than as an established mechanism. And all of it concerns one corpus for
the adult side: ICBHI is the field's default benchmark and the faults we describe are
ICBHI-specific, so the scope of the claim is that benchmark and no further.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We audited our own respiratory sound pipeline on ICBHI 2017 and found four
measurement faults, and we then measured what each was worth rather than assuming they
mattered equally. A macro-averaged variant of the challenge score inflated twenty
committed runs by 0.0948 on average and 0.2176 at worst, and masked two outright model
collapses that the official metric exposes immediately. A silent split fallback
substituted an 11-patient, 7.1\% test partition for the official 40\% one, worth 0.0854
on the best model. The published official split assigns recordings rather than patients
and is not patient-independent, which places 4.4\% of its test cycles in patients the
model may have trained on---but repairing that leakage moved the score by 0.0039, well
inside the run's own confidence interval, so we report it as a validity fault and
explicitly not as a source of inflation. The same split file names a recording whose
device suffix does not match the released audio, so a pipeline joining on the file stem
drops it without saying so. Ranking the faults honestly is part of the contribution: two
of the four change the number materially and two do not. We
supplied corrected baselines under the official metric, a pre-registered
concept-validity gate that was run twice and failed twice under a threshold chosen so
that it \emph{could} fail, and a reading of that failure against a reference standard
that seven senior physicians reproduce at a 47.77\% ICBHI score. Two probes then located that failure. The acoustic information those
detectors were built to capture is present and linearly accessible in a foundation model
that has never heard a stethoscope, and adapting that model to the benchmark degrades
thirteen of the fourteen concepts, worst of all the one concept that carries diagnostic
signal. A supervised probe on that same unadapted representation then reached 0.71 and
0.76 on the crackle and wheeze labels, on the identical cycles the gate failed on and
above the threshold it set. We therefore withdrew the reading we had placed on our own
negative result: these labels are learnable, label reliability is not the binding
constraint, and our detectors were weak. What replaces it is a sharper comparison---a
self-consistent physician agrees with the same cycle labels at $\kappa=0.035$ on
crackles, so this reference standard is reproduced better by a spectrogram model than by
a trained ear. We withdrew two further results of our own: an interpretability cost
recorded from a single random initialisation reversed sign across five, and a mechanism
verdict was being produced by a sign test whose design floor made it incapable of ever
supporting the hypothesis. The contribution is a measurement rather than a mechanism,
and we release the corrected split loader, the canonical score format with paired
significance tests, and the audit tool that detects all three faults.

Three directions follow. The first is to finish the matched re-run: the best model has
now been evaluated on all three treatments of the partition, which is what allowed the
two split faults to be separated, but the remaining backbones have not, and until they
are the fallback comparison stays confounded with checkpoint selection. The same
exercise would also settle whether the operating-point difference we report against the
literature---comparable sensitivity, deficient specificity---is a property of this
backbone and loss or of the corrected partition itself. The second follows from the gap between what a model recovers from these labels and
what a clinician reproduces of them: a multi-rater study with published calibration
exemplars would establish whether that gap is a property of this rater, of the
annotation protocol, or of auscultation by ear, and would settle whether the
fine-versus-coarse crackle distinction should be retired from concept-based work on this
corpus. The third is applying the audit to the wider literature: the three fault
classes described here are detectable from a committed confusion matrix and a stated
partition, and a survey of how many published ICBHI systems supply either would
establish whether these are our errors or the field's.
